## 1. My game (3 points)

My game is called *Jungle Scream Challenge*. To play, you allow microphone access and shout "BANANA!" whenever a banana appears in the jungle. You have to stay silent when a coconut appears, and the game gets progressively faster as you advance through the levels.

## 2. What I changed (3 points)

1. **Game Title:** I changed the `title` setting in `SETTINGS` from "In the Canopy" to "Jungle Scream Challenge" to give the game a different identity.

2. **Game Instructions:** I modified the `rules` text in `SETTINGS` to emphasize shouting rather than simply saying "banana". I wanted the instructions to make the microphone-based gameplay more obvious.

3. **Microphone Feedback:** I modified the feedback messages to encourage players to shout louder when their voice wasn't reaching the required threshold. I also changed the starting message to make the objective clearer.

## 3. My guess, then my data (2 points)

**My guess (written before playing):**

I predicted that I would miss more bananas when they were deeper in the jungle or heavily covered by leaves. I also expected my accuracy to decrease as the levels became faster, since I would have less time to recognize the fruit and respond.

**What happened:**

I completed 31 trials and finished with a score of 195 points. I correctly responded to 24 out of 31 trials, giving me an overall accuracy of 77.4%.

My accuracy decreased as the game progressed:

- Level 1: 90% (9/10)
- Level 2: 80% (8/10)
- Level 3: 70% (7/10)
- Level 4: 0% (0/1)

I also noticed that I correctly avoided all 9 coconuts, but missed 7 out of 22 bananas. My average recorded reaction time was approximately 714 milliseconds.

Interestingly, I was correct on only 5 out of 10 trials at depth 3, compared to 11 out of 12 at depth 1. This was consistent with my prediction that deeper objects would be harder to identify.

## 4. One reason not to trust it (1 point)

One limitation is that I was the only participant and completed just one session. My performance could have been affected by practice, fatigue, or differences in how quickly I reacted, so the results might not represent how other people would perform.
