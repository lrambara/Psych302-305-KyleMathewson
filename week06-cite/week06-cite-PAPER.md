# Week 6 — Citation and Paper Summary

## Part 1: Paper Reference

**Reference:**

Castel, A. D., Pratt, J., & Drummond, E. (2005). The effects of action video game experience on the time course of inhibition of return and the efficiency of visual search. *Acta Psychologica, 119*(2), 217–230. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2005.02.004

**Research question:**

Do experienced action video game players respond faster and perform visual search tasks more efficiently than non-video game players?

**Method:**

The researchers compared action video game players with non-video game players using target detection and visual search tasks. They examined how quickly participants responded to visual targets and whether gaming experience was associated with differences in attention.

**Dependent measure:**

Reaction time, measured in milliseconds (ms), when detecting or responding to visual targets.

**Summary:**

This study examined whether people who regularly play action video games perform differently on visual attention tasks compared with people who do not play these games. The researchers conducted two experiments involving target detection and visual search tasks of varying difficulty. They measured participants' reaction times to determine how quickly they responded to visual information. The results showed that experienced video game players generally responded faster than non-video game players. However, both groups demonstrated similar patterns of inhibition of return, suggesting that they used similar attentional processes. The researchers suggested that faster stimulus-response mapping may explain the gaming group's advantage. This research relates to my Valorant reaction-time project because both investigate how quickly individuals detect and respond to visual stimuli.
